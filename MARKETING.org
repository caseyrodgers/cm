#+TITLE: Catchup Math — Marketing Ideas
#+STARTUP: overview

Doc index: [[file:PROJECT.org][PROJECT.org]]. Grounded in the actual legacy
business model (=src/main/webapp=: pricing pages, faculty-friendly page,
admin getting-started guide) and the real new capabilities landing in
[[file:cm_re/][cm_re]] (the rewrite) — not generic EdTech-marketing filler.

* Where this starts from (context, not guessed)

- Existing business model, real and already running: *B2B* school/
  college licensing (=cm-purchase.html=, a School/College Price List,
  per-student licenses, a *free 60-day pilot* program with no
  obligation) plus *B2C* self-pay personal licenses (3- and 12-month
  terms) and a college self-pay tier.
- Existing selling points already in the legacy marketing pages
  (=faculty_friendly.html=): ease of self-registration (a school
  login name + a program-name password, or =QUIZME= for
  diagnostic-quiz placement), the online whiteboard as *qualitative*
  assessment ("see their work, not just their answer"), and a
  support-heavy pitch (account managers, a two-page getting-started
  guide, teacher FAQ, training videos).
- What's *new* in the rewrite (=cm_re=) that the legacy marketing
  never had to sell: a real installable offline PWA (works with zero
  connectivity once a subject is downloaded — a genuine Chromebook/
  spotty-wifi story, not a claim), live AI-tutor explanations tuned
  to a chosen grade level ("Learn — explain this problem") with
  follow-up Q&A, an AI-graded whiteboard trio (sketch a starting
  point, check my work, read back what I wrote), timed exam-simulation
  practice tests, and per-chapter mastery tracking that turns raw
  stats into "focus here next."
- Net: the rewrite isn't just a technical modernization, it's a
  genuinely new pitch — "the AI tutor that still works when the wifi
  doesn't" — that the current site and sales materials don't say yet.

* The core opportunity: re-launch the pitch, not just the app

The single highest-leverage marketing move is timing a *relaunch
narrative* to the rewrite's ship date rather than quietly swapping the
tech underneath the same old pages. "Same Catchup Math, quietly
rebuilt" wastes the best story available. Concretely:

- A dated "What's New" / relaunch page (and matching email to every
  existing school + personal-license customer) leading with AI
  explanations + true offline support, not "we modernized our stack."
- Retire "GWT-era" screenshots and language on the marketing site —
  anything visual (login flow, whiteboard, subject picker) should be
  re-shot from =cm_re= before any campaign spends money driving
  traffic to a page showing the old UI.

* B2B — schools, districts, colleges

- *Revive and re-market the free 60-day pilot*, but lead with the new
  hook: "no wifi, no problem — try it in the one classroom/lab where
  your Chromebooks always drop signal." IT/purchasing objections
  about offline reliability are a real, specific pain point this
  product now actually solves; say so by name.
- *One-page "what changed for administrators" sheet* alongside the
  existing two-page Getting Started Guide — reassure existing
  licensees the rewrite doesn't change their rostering/login flow
  (school login name + program-name password, =QUIZME= placement)
  even though the UI is new.
- *Case study from an existing pilot/customer*, specifically
  quantifying the whiteboard's "qualitative assessment" angle plus a
  chapter-mastery screenshot — "we could finally see where kids were
  actually stuck, not just their final score."
- *District/state math-intervention or ESSER-style funding angle*:
  remedial/catch-up math products often qualify for intervention
  budget lines separate from general curriculum spend — worth a
  one-pager mapping Catchup Math's features to common intervention
  funding criteria (progress monitoring, diagnostic placement,
  individualized pacing).
- *Conference/webinar presence* timed to the relaunch — the legacy
  page already promises "we'll do webinars," so a live "see the new
  AI tutor" webinar for existing account managers' contact lists is a
  near-zero-cost win using a channel that already exists.

* B2C — parents, students, homeschoolers, self-pay adults

- *Homeschool-specific landing page.* Homeschool families are a
  strong existing fit for "offline PWA + AI tutor tuned to grade
  level + timed practice tests" and are a distinct, addressable
  community (homeschool co-ops, curriculum fairs, homeschool
  Facebook groups/forums) mostly untouched by the current B2B-leaning
  marketing.
- *"Placement Test" as a free top-of-funnel hook* — the product
  already ships a =placement= subject module. A free, no-signup
  "find out what grade level you're actually at" placement quiz is a
  classic lead magnet for the anxious-parent search query ("is my
  kid behind in math").
- *Back-to-school and post-report-card seasonality* — paid search /
  social spend concentrated at back-to-school (Aug–Sep) and right
  after report cards go out (a natural "my kid needs help NOW"
  moment), rather than spread flat all year.
- *Summer-slide positioning* — "catch up before next year starts" is
  almost the product's name already; a dedicated summer campaign
  writes itself.
- *Before/after screenshots of the AI "Learn" explanation* set to a
  student's own grade level — this is genuinely novel enough
  (competitors mostly show static worked solutions) to be the hero
  visual in ads, not buried in a features list.

* Content & SEO

- *"Is my [grade] grader behind in math" / "math placement test
  online free"* type search terms map directly to the free placement
  quiz above — high-intent, low-competition long-tail queries most
  big EdTech players don't bother targeting specifically.
- *Parent-facing blog/content on specific stuck points* ("why does my
  kid get quadratic equations but not word problems") that quietly
  demonstrates the AI explanation feature via embedded examples.
- *Teacher-facing content* on using diagnostic placement + chapter
  mastery for intervention groups — repurpose existing admin
  documentation into shareable, SEO-indexed pages instead of
  PDF/gated guides only existing customers see.

* Partnerships & channels

- *Tutoring centers and learning centers* as a channel, not just a
  competitor — offer Catchup Math as the "homework/practice between
  sessions" companion product a tutoring center can white-label or
  bundle, extending value between paid in-person sessions.
- *School counselors / intervention specialists* as a distinct buyer
  persona from classroom teachers — they're often the ones actually
  searching for remediation tools for specific struggling students.
- *Homeschool curriculum bundlers/co-ops* — cross-listing or bundle
  deals with complementary homeschool curriculum providers.

* Low-cost / quick-win tactics

- *Re-engage every existing licensee* (school + personal) with the
  relaunch email above — zero acquisition cost, warm audience,
  natural renewal-conversation trigger.
- *Testimonials/reviews refresh* — solicit updated reviews/quotes
  from existing pilot schools specifically about the new AI features,
  for both the website and any paid-search ad copy.
- *"Free pilot" as the ad itself* — a B2B paid-search/LinkedIn
  campaign whose entire offer is the existing no-obligation 60-day
  pilot, which already removes the biggest objection (cost, without
  proof) for a district considering a new tool.

* Bigger bets (longer-term, needs more validation first)

- *Freemium self-pay tier* — a limited free version (e.g. one subject,
  or placement-test-only) to build a self-serve funnel parallel to
  the school-sales motion, letting individual parents/students
  convert without ever talking to an account manager.
- *App-store-style PWA install prompts* leaning into "installs like a
  real app, no App Store needed" — useful messaging specifically for
  school IT departments that restrict native app installs but allow
  PWAs.
- *Referral program* for existing personal-license families — a
  "give a free month, get a free month" mechanic is cheap to build
  and fits a self-pay subscription model well.

* Messaging / positioning starting points

- "The math tutor that still works when the wifi doesn't."
- "Show your work — an AI whiteboard tutor that checks *how* you got
  there, not just the answer."
- "Find out exactly where you're stuck, in five minutes, for free."
  (placement-quiz lead magnet)
- "Catch up before the school year catches up with you." (summer-slide
  angle)

* Things to be careful about

- Don't oversell "AI" in a way the product can't back up live — the
  AI explanation/whiteboard-feedback paths already have a documented
  placeholder fallback when no API key / service is unavailable
  (see =AiService.java=); any campaign demo must be run against a
  live-key environment, not the placeholder path, or it undersells
  the feature by showing the degraded state.
- Any offline/Chromebook claim in ad copy should be verified against
  the actual current e2e-tested behavior before it ships in a
  campaign — see [[file:IDEAS.org][IDEAS.org]] and
  [[file:NEW_DIRECTION.org][NEW_DIRECTION.org]] for what's actually
  shipped vs. still in progress at any given time.
- FERPA/COPPA/student-data messaging matters a lot more to B2B
  (district) buyers than B2C parents — any new "AI" messaging aimed
  at schools should be paired with a plain-English data-handling
  statement (what's sent to the AI service, what's stored where),
  since "AI" + "student data" is now a default objection in district
  procurement conversations.

* Patreon — does it fit?

Honest take: *not as a replacement for the core product's revenue*,
but it could work as a genuine parallel layer for the B2C/homeschool
audience specifically. Worth separating what Patreon is actually good
at from what this business already does.

** Where it's a real mismatch

- Patreon is a creator-support platform (patrons back a person/project
  they feel connected to) — it doesn't touch the *B2B* motion at all.
  No district or college is going to license software through a
  Patreon tier; that channel stays exactly as it is (direct
  sales/pilot/purchase).
- It takes a real cut on top of payment processing (Patreon's platform
  fee + Stripe/PayPal fees), which is worse economics than the
  existing direct 3-/12-month self-pay licenses for what would be the
  same underlying transaction if it just replaced them outright.
- Gating actual app *access* behind Patreon membership tiers needs a
  real integration (Patreon's API can verify membership) — and that
  runs straight into an actual architectural decision already made in
  =cm_re=: the tutor is deliberately *account-free*, IndexedDB/
  localStorage only (see IDEAS.org's "Progress export/import" idea,
  whose whole rationale is "no backend account system"). Patreon-gated
  subject access would mean introducing real accounts/auth just to
  check tier status — a bigger architectural change than the marketing
  idea itself, not a quick win.
- Patreon has weak cold-audience discovery. It monetizes people who
  already found you somewhere else; it doesn't replace the SEO/
  placement-quiz/paid-search funnel already proposed above.

** Where it could genuinely work

Not "sell the app on Patreon" — "support free public math content,
with patron perks," running *alongside* the real product, aimed
squarely at the homeschool/parent audience already identified as
underserved by current marketing:

- A small public library of free content (worked-example videos,
  printable practice sheets — reusing the "Printable practice sheets"
  IDEAS.org backlog item, short "why does my kid get X but not Y"
  explainers) funded by patrons, separate from the licensed product.
- Patron perks that don't require gating the app itself: early access
  to new subject modules' release notes, a monthly "request a topic"
  explainer, printable worksheet packs, a patron-only Q&A/office-hours
  call — all things that live *outside* the account-free tutor, so no
  architecture change is needed.
- This works best if there's an actual person/personality attached —
  "watch us build a math-help tool and get extra material along the
  way" reads as authentic on Patreon; "support our SaaS product" does
  not. Only pursue this if someone's willing to be the visible
  face/voice of that content stream.
- Low commitment to test: this can be piloted with zero product
  changes — a handful of free videos/worksheets and a Patreon page,
  see if the homeschool audience actually bites, before building
  anything.

** Bottom line

Patreon is a plausible *top-of-funnel/community* experiment for the
homeschool segment, not a monetization model for the product itself —
and definitely not something that should influence the B2B pricing/
licensing model at all. Treat it as a small, cheap test of the
homeschool content-marketing angle above, not a pivot.
